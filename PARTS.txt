==============================================================================
  HOW EACH JOINT IS MADE
==============================================================================

  roll servo -> shoulder   x4
      4x M3x40 into the horn
      horn pocket + 4 holes on a 15 mm circle, through the roll collar

  pitch servo -> thigh   x4
      4x M3x16 into the horn
      same pattern, through the thigh's hip collar -- which is SOLID, so the horn seats on a face instead of hanging off the screws

  knee servo -> crank   x4
      4x M3x20 into the horn
      same pattern, through the crank's hub

  thigh -> shank (the knee)   x4
      1x M4 shoulder bolt, 5 mm dia x 10 mm
      plain shank turns in the thigh, threads into an M4 insert in the shank

  crank -> pushrod   x4
      1x M3 shoulder bolt, 4 mm dia x 6 mm
      rod turns on the shoulder, threads into an M3 insert in the crank

  pushrod -> rocker   x4
      1x M3 shoulder bolt, 4 mm dia x 6 mm
      same again, into the shank's rocker

  chassis front -> rear   x1
      6x M3x12 + M3 nut, plus two printed snap latches
      halved lap: click the halves together, then bolt. The latches hold it square while you do -- they are not the joint

  servo -> bracket   x12
      4x M3x12 + M3 heat-set insert
      through the filler, through the servo's ear, into the bracket

==============================================================================
  ELECTRONICS  --  no servo hub; the MCU drives them direct
==============================================================================
   qty   part                                what for
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------
     1   Teensy 4.1                          35 PWM pins -- drives all 12 servos with no hub. Hardware float, which the IK wants: trig and roots, twelve times a tick at 200 Hz
     1   BNO055 IMU (or ICM-20948)           the robot BALANCES; the body-pose solver needs attitude. The BNO fuses on-chip, which saves writing a filter for the first build
     1   6 V UBEC, 15 A cont. / 30 A burst   NOT optional: a 2S pack is 7.4-8.4 V and the servo is rated 4.8-6.8. Sized for 10 A transient -- the 3 A hobby UBECs will cook
     2   4700 uF 16 V low-ESR electrolytic   across the servo rail. Twelve servos starting together is a current step the regulator alone will not hold up through
     1   2S LiPo, 5000 mAh                   140 x 46 mm, strapped through the spine
     1   XT60 pair                           battery to the distribution bus
     1   Switch, 30 A                        sized for the stalled case, not the walking one
     1   Fuse + holder, 30 A                 between the pack and everything else
    12   Servo extension lead, 300 mm        hip to the boards, one per servo
     2   Hookup wire, 16 AWG                 metres, red and black -- the servo rail
     1   Hookup wire, 24 AWG                 metre -- signal, to the Teensy

  Current, from the gait rather than the stall rating:
        7.5 A   all twelve, continuous
       10.4 A   realistic transient
         31 A   all twelve stalled, which is what the
              switch and fuse have to survive

  LATER, OR INSTEAD:
    Raspberry Pi 5, 8 GB
        the AI tier, when you get there. Talks to the Teensy over USB serial; it does NOT drive servos
    Jetson Orin Nano
        instead of the Pi, if you want real NN throughput rather than classical CV
    PCA9685 16-channel hub
        ONLY if you drop the MCU and drive the servos from a Pi. One, not two -- 16 channels covers 12. Run it at 333 Hz: at 50 Hz its 12 bits work out to 0.66 deg a step, which is 2.8 mm at the foot

  Insert pilots in the model: M3 4.0 mm, M4 5.6 mm.
  CHECK YOUR OWN INSERTS -- pilot sizes vary by brand, and
  reprinting four thighs because the inserts spin is a bad day.
  CHECK YOUR OWN SERVO too: the 40 x 20 x 40.4 body is near
  universal, the 49.5 mm ear spacing is not.
